# #838 evidence: why the `test` gate timed out, and the arms behind the fix

Measured 2026-10-02 on one 12-core / 96 GB host, against the 756,263,781-byte
aggregate `graphify-out/graph.json`. Every number below was measured in this
round. None is inherited.

`--durations` over the graph-touching modules, run serially at load ~41, found
five tests that parse the real graph:

|---|---|
| `test_eval_cases::test_the_retrieval_case_skips_without_the_slow_flag` | 31.16 |

The first test parsed the graph only to read one row, so it was narrowed to the
case it asserts on.

## Why fix 1 was not enough

`kb-ship` at 165fc872 then failed `tier1.graph-answers` with "graphify query
rc=-1: timed out after 180s" while host load went 10 -> 93-107. The cause was
other sessions, not our gates. A 180 s wall bound on a 31.7 CPU-s query fails
whenever the query gets less than ~18% of a core, whether or not anything is
wrong. The bound was measuring the host.

## Fix 2: bound the work, not the clock

`evals.run_command_cpu_bounded` replaces the wall bound with two checks:
- a CPU budget of 180 CPU-s over the process group, which catches a runaway
- a minimum progress of 1 CPU-s per 60 s, which catches the 0%-CPU wedge

The starvation arm uses SIGSTOP/SIGCONT duty-cycling on the real canary. That
takes CPU away from the query exactly as a saturated run queue does, but spends
none on the shared host.

| bound | duty | wall | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| old: 180 s wall | 0.12 | 155.7 s | pass (too gentle to reproduce) |
| old: 180 s wall | 0.06 | 180.2 s | **FAIL**: timed out after 180s |
| new: 180 CPU-s + stall | 0.06 | 222.7 s | **PASS**: rc=0, 6656 bytes |

The "old" mode is `graphify_canary(timeout=180)`, the same code path 165fc872
used.

Unit arms in `tests/test_cpu_bounded.py`, all rc 0:
- a wedge is killed as stalled in under 10 s
- a runaway is killed on its budget
- a forked child's CPU counts, where a per-pid sample would miss it
- a starved spin passes under the CPU bound, while the same command under a 3 s
  wall bound fails (its control)

The case-wiring test was armed: reverting `tier1.graph-answers` to the wall
bound gives rc 1, and restoring it gives rc 0.
