The 116-Second Blackout

Two cold review lanes finished. The session that dispatched them died in the same second as the first one's notification, and its handoff recorded both as dead. Nothing was lost — but the record said it was.

Nothing was lost.

All four subagent lanes dispatched by session 298fdc02 wrote complete reports to disk. The two the handoff called dead are 270 and 353 lines, each ending in a final verdict. The handoff's §8 has been corrected in place, and handoff-check now exits 0.

What the handoff said, and what is on disk

The handoff reasoned from those two wrong values. It concluded that the incremental-write instruction was what separated a lane leaving 119 usable lines from one leaving nothing — a measurement of a difference that did not exist. It then told the next session to treat this round's work-memory as self-reviewed only, because the outside lane that would have checked it had supposedly died. That lane had in fact finished and found no P0s.

Why the record went wrong

Not a lane failure. A timing failure, inside a sixteen-second window.

Two channels, and only one of them died

A lane reports twice, over paths that fail independently. That separation is exactly what agent-report-persistence.md rule 1 exists to create, and it is why this incident cost a correction rather than two re-dispatches.

The correction is on the handoff, not silently over it. §8 now carries a dated CORRECTED block naming the old values, the two timestamps, and an explicit do not re-dispatch either lane — because the failure mode this leaves behind is a future session spending 2 × 900 s re-deriving work already written.

A second defect, found while fixing the first — and my own diagnosis was wrong

Running handoff-check to verify the correction returned RC=1 with six FAILs, each naming a work-memory file that is on disk. I filed #552 blaming resolve.build_index — before control-arming the alternative. That was wrong, and the issue has been rewritten.

What to carry forward

  • A handoff written while lanes are in flight is a claim about the future. §8 asserted two lanes were dead while one had already finished and the other was 99 seconds out. Report a lane's outcome from the file on disk, never from whether its notification arrived.
  • Incremental persistence did its job — and the handoff credited it with a difference that never happened. Both lanes wrote complete reports. The prose explaining why one left 119 lines and the other nothing was a mechanism invented to explain two wrong numbers.
  • File the issue after the control arm, not before. #552's first version named the wrong module and guessed a symbol name (_AUTHORED_SOURCE_DIRS; it is _SOURCES_KEPT, resolve.py:88). One four-row probe refuted the whole diagnosis.
