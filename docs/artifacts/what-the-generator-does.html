What the Generator Does

Today the index is written by hand and drifts. The generator builds it from the notes themselves — so it can't drift, and it can't outgrow its limit.

The problem

Every note's summary is written twice — once in the note, once in the index. Two copies of the same sentence, kept in step by hand. They fall out of step, and the index grows until it quietly stops being read at all.

The fix

Stop writing the second copy. A small program reads each note's own summary and builds the index from them. One source, so it cannot disagree with itself, and nothing can be missing from it.

Why it fits

Older entries move to a second list next door. Only one list is read at the start of a session; the other sits beside it, costing nothing, holding every older summary in full. Nothing is deleted and nothing becomes unfindable.

The catch

The short list has to say, in plain words, where the long one is. A list nobody is told about is the same as a list that doesn't exist — that is exactly how 64 notes fell off the current one.
