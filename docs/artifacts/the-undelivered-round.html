The Undelivered Round

The Claude ↔ Codex handoff is not slow because the models are slow. One round spent 222.4 minutes waiting on work that had already finished.

Ray put the problem this way, and the wording is what made it answerable:

“one thing we are struggling w is the communication between claude and codex models which seems to be fragile and slow” Ray, 2026-08-27

“Fragile and slow” is two problems, and neither one is the model. Splitting them was the whole job. The evidence is not the published literature — it is 159 cross-family review reports and 235 lane reports already sitting on disk in this repo, every one of them a Claude ↔ Codex handoff that already happened.

The failure is one arrow, and it is the wrong one

A lane returns its result twice: it writes a report to disk, and it sends a notification. The disk write is durable. The notification is the one that fails — and the architect waits on the notification.

That last row deserves its own sentence. The cross-family review policy cannot be audited. report_path strips the :variant, so 157 of 159 reports cannot tell you which model family actually reviewed the code — the transport erases the single attribute the policy exists to enforce.

The cheapest fix is one sentence

The nudge class has a control arm attached, which is what makes it a finding rather than an anecdote. Eight of twenty subagents had to be told their report had not arrived — and twelve were spawned and never nudged. The difference is not the harness. It is the prompt.

prompt saysnudges needed
“write the full report to <path> and also return it as your final message”0 of 12
“return the full report as your final message”8 of 8

The resends cost 109,165 bytes of re-emitted payload — roughly 27k tokens — pushed back into the architect's context, in a session that was ended because context was full.

When to offload to Codex at all

Ray asked for a rule, not a mode setting. The rule the evidence supports: offload when the work is bounded by a spec you can write in full, and the result is verifiable without your context. A diff review against a fixed ref. An implementation whose acceptance criteria already exist. A mechanical migration.

Keep it inline when writing the spec would cost more than doing the work, or when the result can only be judged against conversation the lane will not have. The published guidance says the same thing in the general case — decompose by context boundary, not by role type; splitting planner / implementer / tester / reviewer creates a telephone game where every handoff loses fidelity.

Where a scarce Fable budget buys something

Three named decision points, and nowhere else: before a change that is expensive to reverse; when the same problem has resisted two attempts; and when two lanes disagree and the disagreement has to be adjudicated. Not as the default architect — the architect's expensive work here was re-reading, which a typed contract removes rather than a stronger model.

No new roles

That is a conclusion, not a default. A six-agent roster shipped 2026-08-06 and five of the six match its own research proposal exactly. What changed since is the lane world, not the work: Gemini is now a one-call reserve rather than a routine reviewer, and grok is not installed. Neither change adds a role; both narrow where an existing one may run.

One divergence is undocumented and stays Ray's call: kb-advisor (fable) sits where the synthesis proposed kb-fallback-reviewer (opus), and that synthesis said fable was “deliberately ABSENT from the standing roster”.
