Extraction architecture · demolition plan

Two extraction paths.
Only one is real.

The repo has two ways to turn source files into graph nodes. One drives the graphify CLI. The other re-implements what that CLI already does inside. Your ruling: the second one goes.

The picture in one line

A kitchen with two cooks

You hired a chef (the graphify CLI). Somewhere along the way the kitchen also grew a second cook who watched the chef and rebuilt his recipes from scratch — his own knife work, his own timings, his own receipts.

Both cooks produce food. But now nobody knows which one made dinner, the second cook's recipes drift every time the chef learns something new, and his recipes just broke because the chef changed how he slices.

Fire the second cook. Keep the chef, and keep the waiter who carries plates to the table.

Why now, concretely

The drift already bit

graphify shipped “slice every text document type, not just five of them” and added .html to its splittable list. A 1.85 MB file that used to arrive as one unit now arrives as ~93. The old layer's own assumption — that one catalogue entry equals one unit — stopped being true, and 24 tests went red.

Those 24 tests live entirely in the layer being deleted. Retiring the layer retires the failure, which is why this ruling settles the question I was about to ask you separately.

The one open question

What happens to the receipts?

The old path spent real money on provider calls, and it kept the receipts: 105 files, ~1.97 MB, about $41.78 of paid extraction, tracked in git on purpose. corpus_integrity.py is the gate that checks those receipts still hash to what was recorded.

The catch: that gate is currently red and cannot go green on its own. One chunk is off by −1 byte — the file hashes to e0ec3c17891f while every receipt says b1f2713d4469. The receipts agree with each other, so the file was altered after staging. Nothing regenerates it: the path that made it is being deleted.
Option 1 · recommended

Keep the receipts, keep the gate

You retain the audit trail of what was paid for, and a fresh clone can still verify it. Matches #317, settled in favour of tracking.

Cost: the gate stays red until someone restores that one byte. A permanently-red ship gate trains people to ignore it.

Option 2

Keep the receipts, drop the gate

The 105 files stay in git as a historical record; kb-corpus-integrity leaves GATE_TASKS.

Why it fits: a gate guards something that can still change. Nothing will ever write these files again, so it is guarding a museum.

Option 3

Delete both

Full retirement — the path is gone, so its receipts are archaeology. Removes the red gate and ~2 MB.

Irreversible: provider evidence is not regenerable at these digests. You would be spending $41.78 of proof for 2 MB.

One combination is a trap, so it is not offered: deleting the evidence while keeping the gate. With zero chunks to scan, integrity_main() returns Rc.NOT_RUN rather than success — by design, because a gate that never asked the question is not a pass. It would sit permanently red on the ship path.
