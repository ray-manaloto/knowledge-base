What Is Aggregated Research For?

Seven decisions settled how to build it and where it lives. None of them said what a person gets by invoking it. This page holds the two pictures that were in disagreement, the tree of decisions that hangs off that gap, and what each round settled. rounds 1–4 + the advisor: 14 settled — every branch of the tree visited, nothing waiting 3 settled — HOW, not WHAT

Two pictures of one plugin

A is what the last session was building toward: a sequential sweep, cheapest step first, that stops when a cheap step settles the question, with a control arm on every null. B is what you described in the artifact comments: the pipeline shape of /deep-research, fanned out over all our tools at once, with a vote on every claim. The blue boxes are where they differ.

A costs less
A cheap step that settles the question ends the run. B always pays for the fan-out.
B trusts less
B never lets one source be the answer — three votes per claim. A trusts the cheapest source that discriminates.
Same rule, different depth
A puts never asked ≠ answered no at each probe. B puts it in the tally: too few valid votes is unverified, never refuted.

Q1

What does a person get by invoking it?

The one thing the parts do not give on their own.

A
A verified answer. Picture B: fan out, dedup, fetch, vote, synthesize. The plugin is the pipeline over our tools.
B
A disciplined sweep. Picture A: the ordering plus the control arm plus the report shape. A checklist the agent walks.
C
Both, nested. B is the discipline inside each source step of A; the vote stage is where never asked ≠ answered no lives.
➡️ C. Your comment 0bd0b4fa asks for the pipeline shape with our tools as its sources. The sweep discipline does not go away — it becomes what each source step must satisfy before its result may vote.

Q2

When does it run?

Directive item 5 asks for a daily morning brief an agent team creates, reviews and synthesizes. #509 describes an on-demand question.

A
On demand only. A person or agent brings one question; the three #509 test prompts are the shape.
B
The daily brief only. A standing question list, scheduled; the answer is a brief, not a reply.
C
Both, one pipeline, two entry points. The brief is a scheduled caller of the on-demand run with a standing question list.
➡️ C, on-demand first. A brief that schedules a pipeline that does not yet exist is a cron job with nothing to call. Build the run; then the brief is a routine plus a question list.
SETTLED — round 1: both, on-demand first.
unblocks: selection rule · output · budget

Who is the user?

Decision 3 (staged standalone package) was made assuming "any repo". The answer decides whether graph-first is a hard dependency or an optional first source.

A
This repo only. It may assume kb-query, the mise tasks and the guards. Simplest; the marketplace is then pointless.
B
Any of your repos. dotfiles and siblings, where there is no graph. Step 0 becomes "the graph if one exists".
C
Anyone from the marketplace. Zero of our infrastructure may be assumed; every source is a declared plugin or a public API.
➡️ B. It is what the marketplace decision was already assuming, and it is the smallest scope that makes "install the plugin" a real test. C can be a later hardening, not a first target.

What is /deep-research to it?

It is user-invoked only — no agent can start it (workflows.md:80, and a lane measured it 2026-08-27). Your comment said "use this workflow or steps but with the results from the other tools, and maybe use /deep-research as one of the sources."

A
The shape. Copy its stages and its three-outcome tally as the plugin's spine.
B
A source. One optional breadth input, present only when you type it.
C
Both. The spine is copied; the command is one source that only a person can add, so the daily brief runs without it.
➡️ C — it is what you said, and the one consequence worth confirming: because only you can type it, the scheduled brief can never include it.
SETTLED — round 1: A, the shape only.
