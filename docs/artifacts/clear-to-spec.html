The short answer to “can we move to /to-spec”

Yes — for the slice. Everything needed to specify slice 1 is settled or decidable: the schema, the two pins, the state machine, the generated-docs treatment split.

Not yet for the full protocol. Two of the five below — the state model and the manifest scope — would be baked into the spec as guesses, and both are expensive to unwind after tickets exist. They are worth ten minutes now.

My recommendation: answer the five, then /to-spec the full protocol with slice 1 first in the ticket chain. If you would rather move now, say so and I will spec slice 1 alone under stated assumptions and we settle the rest before slice 2.

The five

Each with a recommendation, so none is a blank page

1 · Does upgrade status survive a clone?

Q9 from round 3, never answered

Your state-machine comment settled the shape — generated enum, a library owning transitions. What is still open is where the SQLite file lives: gitignored and rebuilt from tracked text, or tracked and travelling in the PR.

My recommendation Gitignored, rebuilt from mise.toml / pyproject.toml / the manifests — but each batch writes a small tracked status record. That keeps a binary out of git and avoids merge conflicts on concurrent runs, while the evidence a reviewer needs still survives. The generated-docs slice is already building exactly that renderer, so it costs nothing extra.

2 · The 23 tracked JSON state files

Q10 from round 3, never answered — and the plan’s premise was wrong

The design said “delete the 23 dead JSON files”. They are not dead. A cold lane read them: they hold reviewed findings, timestamped upstream baselines and docs fingerprints, and none maps to the proposed stage enum. Deleting them erases human review acknowledgements.

My recommendation Migrate, do not delete. The review acknowledgements become watch_review rows — which is what makes “cleared against WHICH release” answerable at all. Baselines and fingerprints become probe rows. Delete the files only after a migration proves every value has a home, in a later commit than the one that adds the tables.

3 · What all-generated meant, and the 12

Q12 from round 3 — your answer admits two readings, and collides with a measurement

Two questions in one answer. Scope: does “generated” mean every byte of a manifest, or only the pin fields? sources/graphify.manifest is ~90 lines of authored judgment. The 12: you ruled all 97 come under the protocol, but 12 of them are not dependencies and have nothing to generate from.

My recommendation Generate pin fields only, preserving authored prose — this is what the existing updater already does, so it is also the smallest change. And keep the 12 as stub rows rather than deleting them: they are knowledge sources someone ingested deliberately, and a documented exception is cheaper than losing them.

4 · A red gate — your answer versus your rule

Q6 from round 2, against two eager standing rules

You answered revert-continue: revert that dependency, keep the batch going. zero-skip-policy and verify-before-advancing both say a red check is the current task and nothing advances past it. An implementer cannot be left to pick which of your instructions wins.

My recommendation Scope the rules explicitly: write into zero-skip-policy.md that a rejected upgrade candidate is not a project failure — restore it, record the rejection with evidence, continue. The rules keep full force over the repo’s own code. The point is that the exception gets written down; an unwritten one gets re-litigated by every future session.

5 · Full protocol, or slice 1?

Q13 — and the question you just asked me

You called the MVP “the full upgrade automation with the ability to skip agent tokens”. The advisor’s slice 1 is narrower: two tables, populate, kb-upgrade, every token stage recorded skipped, plus a gate that reads whether mise.lock advanced.

My recommendation /to-spec the full protocol, then let /to-tickets cut it into slices with slice 1 first. Speccing only slice 1 risks freezing a schema the later stages cannot use — and the schema is the expensive thing to get wrong. The spec is cheap; the rework is not.
